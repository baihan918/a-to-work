# Frontend Job-Hop Playbook

Use this file when the user needs a concrete preparation plan, a progress reset, or a reusable template for job search execution.

## 1. Stage Roadmap

### Phase 1: Positioning and target definition

Goal: stop preparing blindly and decide what kind of move is actually worth making.

Checklist:

- Record current baseline:
  - years of experience
  - current company and business type
  - current compensation structure
  - current level / scope
  - promotion or raise ceiling
- Define the upgrade target:
  - higher pay
  - stronger platform
  - better title
  - AI transition
  - platform / efficiency direction
- Write one positioning sentence:
  - avoid "I am a frontend engineer with X years"
  - prefer "I focus on complex business systems / AI landing / engineering efficiency"
- Build the company list:
  - ideal
  - realistic
  - fallback
- Define compensation:
  - anchor
  - target
  - floor
  - walk-away conditions

Exit criteria:

- one clear positioning sentence exists
- one target-role list exists
- one company list with at least 20 entries exists
- one compensation strategy exists

### Phase 2: Technical preparation

Goal: reach the level where technical rounds do not expose obvious weaknesses.

Checklist:

- Algorithm:
  - arrays / hash / two pointers
  - linked lists
  - stacks / queues
  - binary tree traversal
  - BFS / DFS
  - binary search
  - backtracking basics
  - DP template questions
- Frontend fundamentals:
  - browser rendering pipeline
  - event loop and async model
  - closures / scope / this / prototype chain
  - HTTP / cache / cookie / storage
  - CORS / XSS / CSRF
  - reflow / repaint / compositing
- Framework depth:
  - React or Vue runtime mechanism
  - state and data flow
  - lifecycle or effect model
  - performance optimization
  - SSR / hydration basics if relevant
- Engineering and architecture:
  - module boundaries
  - large-project organization
  - TypeScript type design
  - build / release / CI / quality controls
  - monitoring / logging / stability
- AI landing depth:
  - what the workflow is
  - what is automated
  - what the quality gates are
  - where humans still review
  - what the actual measurable improvement is

Deliverables:

- one 2-4 week technical study plan
- one high-frequency question bank
- one weak-spot list
- one mock interview transcript

Exit criteria:

- can solve common medium algorithm questions with explanation
- can explain core frontend topics without obvious gaps
- can explain one architecture decision and one performance case deeply
- can explain AI landing as engineering work rather than tool usage

### Phase 3: Story and material preparation

Goal: make the user sound like a stronger candidate than their job title suggests.

Checklist:

- Resume:
  - rewrite summary around scope and impact
  - remove generic stack laundry list
  - rewrite bullets into action + system + result
- Project stories:
  - pick 5 to 6 stories
  - cover business complexity, engineering depth, and AI landing if available
  - prepare both 2-minute and 5-minute versions
- Self-introduction:
  - 60-90 seconds
  - current role
  - strongest narrative
  - two representative projects
  - why looking now
  - what role next
- Common behavioral answers:
  - biggest challenge
  - conflict and collaboration
  - technical decision tradeoff
  - failure and reflection
  - why leave current company
- Salary talk preparation:
  - current package phrasing
  - expected range phrasing
  - bottom-line phrasing

Deliverables:

- one updated resume
- one self-introduction
- five to six polished stories
- one answer pack for common behavioral questions

Exit criteria:

- resume can be sent immediately
- self-introduction can be delivered naturally
- each project story has context, decision, impact, and reflection
- salary expectation can be stated cleanly

### Phase 4: Application and interview execution

Goal: turn preparation into real interview momentum and offers.

Checklist:

- Start small-scale applications first for feedback.
- Use early interviews to detect weak spots.
- Track:
  - application source
  - stage
  - feedback
  - exposed weak points
  - compensation signal
- Before each important interview:
  - reread company and role JD
  - rematch project stories to that role
  - review likely technical topics
  - rehearse self-introduction and leaving reason
- After each interview:
  - summarize weak points within 24 hours
  - patch the top 1 to 2 issues before the next interview
- Before offer stage:
  - revisit target / floor / tradeable items
  - decide what you will trade and what you will not trade

Deliverables:

- one application tracker
- one interview feedback tracker
- one negotiation script
- one offer comparison sheet

Exit criteria:

- active pipeline exists
- weak spots are being patched based on real feedback
- salary conversation is no longer improvised

## 2. Eight-Week Plan

### Week 1: Baseline and target

- Write down current package, city, role ceiling, and why staying is no longer attractive.
- Choose a target narrative:
  - AI engineering landing
  - frontend platform / engineering efficiency
  - senior business architecture frontend
  - frontend toward full-stack or agent engineering
- Produce:
  - one target-role statement
  - one target salary range
  - one list of 20 companies split into ideal / realistic / fallback

### Week 2: Resume rebuild

- Rewrite the top half of the resume around scope and impact.
- Remove low-value framework laundry lists.
- Turn project bullets into `action + system + impact`.
- Produce:
  - one full resume draft
  - three strongest project bullets rewritten with metrics

### Week 3: Project-story extraction

- Select 5 to 6 projects worth talking about.
- For each project, write:
  - context
  - problem
  - your role
  - design choices
  - tradeoffs
  - result
  - what you would improve now
- Produce:
  - six interview-ready project stories

### Week 4: AI and engineering differentiation

- Separate shallow AI usage from real AI landing.
- Gather evidence for:
  - workflow design
  - model/tool integration
  - coding assistant usage policy
  - evals or quality control
  - team efficiency uplift
- Produce:
  - one AI-focused story deck with 2 to 3 strong examples

### Week 5: Technical interview hardening

- Review core topics most likely to surface:
  - browser and rendering
  - JavaScript / TypeScript depth
  - React or framework internals relevant to your stack
  - performance and stability
  - frontend engineering and release workflow
  - architecture tradeoffs
- Produce:
  - one question bank
  - one mock interview transcript

### Week 6: Market launch

- Start referrals and direct applications.
- Prioritize roles that match the narrative, not only the logo.
- Track every application and response.
- Produce:
  - first 10 to 15 applications
  - first wave of interview feedback

### Week 7: Iteration and negotiation prep

- Use interview feedback to patch weak spots.
- Define:
  - anchor number
  - acceptable range
  - floor
  - walk-away conditions
- Produce:
  - one compensation strategy note
  - one revised self-introduction

### Week 8: Offer conversion

- Continue interviews with focused patching.
- Compare roles using:
  - pay
  - title / level
  - scope
  - growth path
  - manager quality
  - AI relevance
- Produce:
  - one offer comparison sheet
  - one final decision memo

## 3. Technical Preparation Focus

Use this section when the user says "我先做技术准备" or asks what to study first.

### Algorithm survival line

- Spend enough time to avoid failing obvious coding rounds.
- Do not let algorithm practice consume the whole preparation process.
- Default target:
  - 30 to 45 minutes per day
  - 4 to 5 days per week
  - focus on 40 to 80 high-frequency questions

### Frontend fundamentals

Prepare to explain:

- request to render pipeline
- event loop and task queues
- JavaScript object model and closures
- browser cache and HTTP semantics
- rendering, layout, painting, compositing
- security basics and common attack prevention

### Engineering depth

Prepare one strong case for each:

- architecture decision
- performance optimization
- engineering efficiency improvement
- stability / monitoring / quality control

### AI landing depth

Prepare specific answers for:

- why AI was introduced
- what workflows changed
- how output quality is controlled
- where risk exists
- what measurable gains were observed

## 4. Resume Bullet Pattern

Use this structure:

`I led/built/owned X system for Y scenario, solved Z problem, and improved A/B/C metric by N%.`

Examples:

- Owned the merchant-side operational workflow for a core SaaS module, redesigned the frontend state and data flow, and reduced critical-page error rate by 35%.
- Built an AI-assisted coding workflow for the team, standardized prompt, review, and acceptance steps, and cut repetitive development time by about 30%.

## 5. Project Story Template

Use this template for each story:

1. Background: what business or team situation triggered the work
2. Core problem: what was broken, slow, risky, or expensive
3. Ownership: what you specifically owned
4. Solution: architecture, engineering design, tradeoffs
5. Obstacles: cross-team, quality, time, or legacy-system constraints
6. Result: measurable business or engineering outcome
7. Reflection: what you would change now

## 6. Self-Introduction Template

Keep it to 60 to 90 seconds.

Structure:

1. Current role and years
2. Strongest positioning statement
3. Two representative achievements
4. Why you are looking now
5. What role you are targeting next

Example skeleton:

"I have 9 years of frontend experience and spent the last 3 years in a SaaS company. In recent work, I am not just doing page delivery; I focus more on complex business systems and AI-enabled engineering landing. Two projects that represent me well are A and B. I am exploring now because my current role has little room for compensation or title growth, and I want the next step to be a stronger platform where I can take on broader engineering ownership."

## 7. Salary Strategy Template

Define these before formal interviews:

- Anchor: the number you open with
- Target: the range you expect
- Floor: below this, the move is not worth it
- Tradeable items: title, sign-on, bonus multiple, scope, team fit
- Walk-away conditions: low scope, fake title, weak manager, or pay below floor

## 8. Weekly Review Template

Answer these every week:

1. What artifact did I produce this week?
2. What part of my story got stronger?
3. Where did I get blocked?
4. What is the single weakest link now?
5. What will I ship next week?

## 9. Interview Tracker Fields

Track at least:

- company
- role
- city
- level guess
- source
- date applied
- stage
- feedback summary
- weak points exposed
- comp range
- next action
